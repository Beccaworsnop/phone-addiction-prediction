📊 Key Findings & Conclusions

2,401 Algerian students surveyed, covering age, gender, school grade, smartphone habits, daily/weekly usage, sleep, academic performance, and phone purposes (social media, gaming, education, etc.).

The dataset is clean and complete, making it reliable for analysis and predictions.

Addiction Levels:

Extremely high smartphone addiction: mean ≈ 8.9/10, with 75% of students rating 8–10.

Over 1,200 students hit the maximum addiction score (10).

Distribution is heavily skewed toward high addiction levels.

Correlations:

Positive correlations with:

Daily usage hours

Time on social media

Number of apps used daily

Time spent gaming

Phone checks per day

Negative correlation with sleep hours → heavier phone usage = less sleep.

Academic performance, family communication, and demographics showed weak or minimal impact on addiction compared to behavioral factors.

Overall Insight:

Smartphone addiction appears to be behaviorally driven (usage intensity, social media, gaming) rather than demographic or academic.

Most surveyed students exhibit very high levels of phone dependency, which could have implications on sleep and overall well-being.
